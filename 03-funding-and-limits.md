# Funding and limits

Before using a key anywhere, add a spending limit on the provider account, and fund it with a small amount you are willing to spend. The limit is the single most important setup step, because it caps what any usage, or any leak, can cost.

## Set the hard cap first

Most providers let you set a maximum monthly spend, after which usage is blocked. Set this before you paste the key into any tool. The cap turns an open-ended billing risk into a bounded one: even if a tool misbehaves or the key leaks, the damage stops at the cap. People who get a shocking bill are almost always the ones who never set this.

## Fund a small amount

Many providers run on prepaid credit: you add a balance, and usage draws it down. Starting with a small balance, rather than linking a card with unlimited auto-recharge, is a simple way to bound your exposure. You can always add more once you see how much normal use actually costs. A small prepaid balance is its own form of limit.

## Layer alerts under the cap

In addition to the hard cap, set usage alerts at points below it if the provider offers them. Alerts warn you as spend climbs, so you can investigate a problem while there is still room before the cap. The cap is the circuit breaker; the alerts are the early warning. Together they mean usage rarely surprises you.

## Why this comes before everything

Funding and limits come before pasting the key anywhere because the risk exists the moment a key is live. A key with no cap is an open tab; the cap is what closes it to a known maximum. This step takes a couple of minutes and is the difference between a manageable BYOK setup and a potential billing accident. Do it first, every time.
